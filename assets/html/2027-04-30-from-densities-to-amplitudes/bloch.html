Every point of the sphere is ψ = a·e₁ + b·eiγe₂ with a = cos(ϑ/2) and b = sin(ϑ/2): height sets the weights a² and b², and longitude is the relative phase γ. The basis states e₁ and e₂ sit at the poles; choose them with the selector. ψ₀ and ψ₁ can be any two points, and the blue great circle between them is the Fubini-Study geodesic. At each x, ψ(x) is the sum of two arrows, A = a·e₁(x) and B = b·eiγe₂(x), and the density is the squared length of the sum: |A|² + |B|² + 2|A||B| cos Δ. Where the arrows line up the density grows; where they oppose it shrinks. The cross term integrates to zero, since ∫ē₁e₂ = 0. Moving along a latitude changes γ, which turns every arrow B by the same angle.
